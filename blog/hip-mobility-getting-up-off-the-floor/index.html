Hip Mobility: The Key to Getting Up Off the Floor With Ease

Getting down to the floor and back up again is a simple test of how well your body moves. Here's how to keep your hips ready for it.

Playing with grandkids on the living room rug, gardening, stretching after a workout, picking something up from under the table: getting down to the floor and back up is something we do more often than we realize. When it starts to feel difficult, it's often a sign that your hips have stiffened up.

Hips are designed to move in many directions. Years of sitting, driving and limited movement can tighten them, which affects how you walk, squat, bend and balance. The encouraging part is that hip mobility usually improves with a few minutes of regular practice.

Signs your hips could use some attention

  • Stiffness when you stand up after sitting for a while
  • Difficulty putting on socks and shoes
  • Struggling to get down to or up from the floor
  • Low back tightness after long days at a desk
  • A shorter stride when you walk

Five hip mobility moves

Move slowly and stay within a comfortable range. You should feel a stretch, never sharp pain. Use a chair or wall for support as needed.

  • Hip flexor stretch: half-kneeling, squeeze the back glute and shift forward, 30 seconds per side
  • 90/90 switches: sit with both knees bent to one side, then rotate them to the other side, 6 to 8 times
  • Supported deep squat hold: hold onto a counter and sink into a comfortable squat for 20 to 30 seconds
  • Figure-four stretch: lying on your back, cross one ankle over the opposite knee and gently pull the legs in, 30 seconds per side
  • Standing leg swings: hold a wall and swing one leg forward and back 10 times, then side to side

Practice getting down and up

One of the best ways to keep the ability to get off the floor is simply to practice it. Once a day, lower yourself to the floor in whatever way feels safe, then stand back up. Use a sturdy chair or couch for help at first. Over time, try using a little less support. It's a skill, and skills improve with repetition.

Mobility and strength go together

Mobility gives you range; strength lets you control it. Squats, lunges, step-ups and hip hinges all build the leg and hip strength that makes getting up easier. The best results come from combining both.

Make it part of your routine

Pick two or three of the moves above and do them most days, perhaps first thing in the morning or during a break from sitting. A few minutes is enough. With so much time spent sitting around the table this holiday weekend, it's a great time to start.

If you've had a hip replacement or hip injury, or have ongoing hip pain, check with your doctor or physical therapist about which movements are right for you.
