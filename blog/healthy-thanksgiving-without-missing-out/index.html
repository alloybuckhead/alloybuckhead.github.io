Enjoying Thanksgiving Without Losing Your Progress

Thanksgiving is one meal, not a setback. A few simple habits let you enjoy the holiday fully and feel good the next morning.

Thanksgiving is about family, gratitude and, yes, a lot of good food. If you've been working on your strength and health, it's natural to wonder how one big holiday meal fits in. The answer is reassuring: one day of eating well beyond your usual won't undo weeks of training. What matters is what you do on most days.

Here's how to enjoy the holiday without the guilt or the food coma.

Don't skip meals to "save up"

Arriving at dinner starving is the fastest way to overeat and feel uncomfortable afterward. Have a normal breakfast with some protein, like eggs or Greek yogurt, and a light lunch or snack if dinner is late. You'll enjoy the meal more and make choices you feel good about.

Start the morning with movement

A morning walk, a short strength routine or a neighborhood turkey trot sets a good tone for the day. Many Atlanta families make a walk part of the holiday tradition. It's a great way to spend time together before the cooking and the football take over.

Build a balanced plate

  • Start with turkey or another protein
  • Add vegetables and salads
  • Then pick the sides you truly love, rather than a little of everything
  • Save room for the dessert that matters most to you

Eating slowly helps too. It takes time for your body to register that you're full, so put your fork down between bites and enjoy the conversation.

Be mindful with drinks

Alcohol, cider and festive cocktails add up quickly and can leave you feeling sluggish. Alternate drinks with water and decide ahead of time roughly what you'll have.

Take a walk after the meal

A 10- to 20-minute walk after a big meal can help you feel less sluggish and is a nice way to get some fresh air with family. Invite whoever's willing and make it part of the tradition.

Get right back to your routine

The most important part of the holiday is the day after. Skip the "I'll start again Monday" mindset. Have a normal, protein-rich breakfast, drink plenty of water, and keep your next scheduled workout. Leftovers are fine; just build your plate the same way you would on any other day.

Practice gratitude for your body

Thanksgiving is also a good moment to appreciate what your body lets you do: carry the groceries, play with the kids or grandkids, walk after dinner. Strength training is about protecting those everyday joys for years to come.
