No Time? How to Get Real Results From Shorter Workouts

You don't need 90 minutes in the gym to get stronger. Focused, well-planned sessions deliver more than long, wandering ones.

"I don't have time" is the most common reason people give for not exercising, and it's a real one. Between work, family, commuting around Atlanta and everything else, finding an extra hour or two can feel impossible.

The good news: strength doesn't require marathon gym sessions. What matters most is how you use the time you have.

Why shorter can work just as well

Long workouts often include a lot of wandering, scrolling between sets and exercises that don't move the needle. A well-planned session focuses on the movements that matter, keeps rest periods purposeful, and gets you out the door. Consistent, focused training two or three times a week beats long, sporadic sessions every time.

Focus on the big movements

When time is short, prioritize exercises that work several muscle groups at once:

  • A squat or lunge for your legs
  • A hinge, like a deadlift variation, for your hips and back
  • A push, like a push-up or press
  • A pull, like a row
  • A carry, holding weights while you walk

Five movements, done well, cover nearly your whole body.

Use smart structure

Pairing exercises saves a lot of time. For example, do a set of squats, then a set of rows, then rest. Your legs recover while your upper body works, so you get more done without rushing. Circuits of three or four exercises work the same way.

Cut the waste, not the warm-up

A few minutes of warm-up is worth keeping, especially as we age. It prepares your joints and helps you lift better. Instead, trim the parts that don't help: long breaks, phone checks and exercises chosen at random. Having a plan before you start is the biggest time-saver of all.

Make every session count

  • Know what you're doing before you walk in
  • Track your weights so you can progress over time
  • Choose a challenging but manageable effort, finishing each set with a rep or two left in reserve
  • Schedule sessions at the same times each week so you never have to decide

Fit movement into the rest of your day

On non-training days, small bouts add up: a walk after lunch, taking the stairs, a few squats while the coffee brews. These don't replace strength training, but they keep you moving and help you feel better.
