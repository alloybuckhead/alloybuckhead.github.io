On a GLP-1? Why Strength Training Matters More Than Ever

Medications like Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro and Zepbound help the scale move. Strength training makes sure what you lose is mostly fat, not the muscle that keeps you strong.

GLP-1 medications such as semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy) and tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound) have changed weight loss for millions of people. They quiet your appetite, so you eat less and the weight comes off. That's a real win. But there's a part of the story that doesn't get talked about enough: not all of the weight you lose is fat.

What the research shows

When researchers measure body composition in GLP-1 trials, roughly 25 to 40 percent of the weight people lose shows up as lean mass. Lean mass isn't only muscle (it also includes water and organ tissue), but some of it is the muscle you rely on to climb stairs, carry groceries and stay steady on your feet.

This isn't unique to the medication. Any fast weight loss without a plan to protect muscle tends to cost some lean tissue. The encouraging part is that it's largely within your control. In studies where people combined a calorie deficit with resistance training, the share of weight lost as lean mass dropped substantially.

Why muscle matters so much

Muscle is what keeps you capable. It powers every movement, supports your joints, helps manage blood sugar and plays a big role in balance and fall prevention as you age. Losing weight but also losing strength can leave you lighter but less able, and it can make it harder to keep the weight off later.

The three habits that protect your muscle

  • Strength train two to three times a week. Work your whole body: legs, hips, back, chest and arms. This is the signal that tells your body to keep its muscle.
  • Make protein the first thing on your plate. With a smaller appetite, every meal counts. Many experts suggest spreading protein across your meals, with roughly 20 to 30 grams at each one.
  • Track strength, not just the scale. If your weights or reps are holding steady or climbing while the scale drops, you're losing mostly fat.

Training on a GLP-1: what to expect

Some people feel more tired or a little queasy, especially when they first start a medication or change doses. That's not a reason to skip training, but it is a reason to train smart. Start with lighter weights, focus on good form, eat a little protein beforehand and hydrate well. On low-energy days, do fewer sets rather than skipping the session entirely. Consistency beats intensity.

Talk with your prescriber

Your doctor knows your health history, so check with them before starting a new exercise program and ask about a protein target that's right for you. Then build a plan that protects the strength you'll want for decades.
