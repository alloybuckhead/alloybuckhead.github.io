Healthy Shoulders: Mobility for Reaching Overhead With Ease

Reaching the top shelf, lifting a carry-on into the overhead bin, swinging a racket: your shoulders do a lot. Here's how to keep them moving well.

Shoulders are the most mobile joints in the body, which is exactly why they need attention. Hours at a desk, driving and looking at phones pull them forward and tighten the muscles around them. Over time, reaching overhead can start to feel stiff, limited or uncomfortable.

The good news is that shoulder mobility usually responds well to a few minutes of regular, gentle work, especially when it's paired with strength training.

Why shoulders get stiff

  • Rounded posture from sitting and screens
  • A stiff upper back, which limits how far the arms can travel overhead
  • Weak muscles between the shoulder blades
  • Simply not reaching overhead very often in daily life

Notice that several of these have little to do with the shoulder joint itself. Improving upper-back mobility and shoulder-blade strength often makes the biggest difference.

Five moves to try

Move slowly and stay in a comfortable range. You should feel a stretch or effort, never sharp pain.

  • Wall slides: stand with your back to a wall, arms in a goalpost shape, and slowly slide them up and down, 8 to 10 reps
  • Open books: lie on your side with knees bent and arms out in front, then rotate your top arm open toward the floor behind you, 6 to 8 per side
  • Doorway chest stretch: forearms on the doorframe, step through gently and hold 20 to 30 seconds
  • Band pull-aparts: hold a light band at shoulder height and pull it apart by squeezing your shoulder blades, 10 to 12 reps
  • Overhead reach with a breath: sit tall, reach both arms overhead, take a slow breath and lower, 5 reps

Build strength, not just range

Mobility gives your shoulders room to move; strength keeps them stable and protected. Rows, carries, push-ups and controlled overhead presses all help, as long as weights and range are matched to what your shoulders can handle today. This is where good coaching matters: small changes in grip, angle or range can turn an uncomfortable exercise into a productive one.

Make it part of your day

  • Do two or three of the moves above during a work break
  • Set your screen at eye level so you're not hunching forward
  • Reach up and stretch every time you stand up from your desk
  • Carry bags on alternating sides

When to get it checked

If you have pain at night, pain that radiates down your arm, weakness, numbness, or a recent injury, see your doctor or a physical therapist before starting a new routine.
